## Recommendation

Make Openline's launch presentation more explicit rather than more decorative. Lead with “Travel data. Made clear.”, then show destination choice, plan comparison, setup details and data management. Retain Openline orange, black and warm white rather than borrowing another brand's palette or illustration style.

## What the category leaders communicate

### Saily

- **Text:** The US listing opens with roaming bills and airport Wi-Fi, then explains eSIMs, setup, plan choices and support. It also positions security features and a second number as differentiators; these are Saily's claims, not features to assume for Openline. [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/saily-esim-data-for-travel/id6475045151)
- **Graphics:** The inspected first four cards use sky-blue backgrounds, yellow highlights and enlarged devices or plan callouts. Headlines include “Use one eSIM in 200+ destinations”, “Unlimited Plans”, a phone-number message and affordable mobile data. A hand-held phone makes the first image more tangible. [Inspected App Store screenshot strip](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/saily-esim-data-for-travel/id6475045151)
- **Google Play:** Its opening foregrounds mobile data, avoiding roaming fees, setup and connection on arrival. Treat all timing and coverage promises as publisher claims. [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.saily.android&hl=en_US)
- **Openline takeaway:** State the product category immediately; give each image one concrete job. Use evidence from the actual app, not vague lifestyle copy.

### Airalo

- **Text:** “Stay connected anywhere” is supported by its description of plan types, destination breadth, installation steps, FAQs and multiple-eSIM management. Its traveler counts and coverage figures are publisher claims. [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/airalo-esim-for-travel-data/id1475911720)
- **Graphics:** The first cards use lime backgrounds with decorative travel imagery, including a dragon crossing the composition, and large device mockups. “Connection means the world”, destination breadth and “30M” social proof appear early; a later blue card introduces unlimited data. [Inspected App Store screenshot strip](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/airalo-esim-for-travel-data/id1475911720)
- **Google Play:** The description explains local, regional, global and unlimited choices and the sequence from choosing a plan to installation. [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mobillium.airalo&hl=en_US)
- **Openline takeaway:** Use a deliberate sequence and proof. Until Openline has approved quantitative evidence, show clarity of plan information instead of borrowing customer-count or coverage claims.

### Holafly

- **Text:** Unlimited data, trip-duration choices, guided installation and support dominate the descriptions. The App Store subtitle is “Travel SIM Card & WiFi Hotspot”. [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/holafly-esim-unlimited-data/id1629600786)
- **Graphics:** Coral/pink backgrounds, burgundy text blocks and large phones create a consistent, benefit-led set. Visible claims include “Stay connected”, “One eSIM for all your trips”, a “12 GB Backup Plan”, and “What you see is what you pay”. These are observed marketing claims, not independently certified capabilities. [Inspected App Store screenshot strip](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/holafly-esim-unlimited-data/id1629600786)
- **Google Play:** Its description repeats unlimited data, installation guidance, hotspot and multilingual support. Openline should not adopt those promises without verifying its own plans and support operations. [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.holafly.holafly&hl=en_US)
- **Openline takeaway:** Make included services and conditions easy to understand. Repetition can help establish one message, but absolute promises need SKU-level evidence.

### Subsequent icon revision 03

Paul requested another 15–20% reduction from the 52.7% candidate. The current
default is **43.5%**, approximately 17.5% smaller linearly; **44.8%** and **42.2%**
compare approximately 15% and 20% reductions. These values are rounded to one

The Android adaptive candidate also shrinks by 17.5%, from 48dp to 39.6dp on the

user-requested small-size experiment requiring real-device legibility testing.
The native package preserves the previous 48dp foreground and monochrome layers
as a fallback. [Android adaptive-icon guidance](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/system/icon_design_adaptive)

## Keep, improve, verify

- **Keep:** The canonical mark, Openline orange and Play/Inter typography, restrained layouts and no baked-in icon corners or shadows.
- **Improve next:** Replace the low-priority account-preferences sixth image with a verified help, compatibility or installation-success screen when available. Do not invent a screen or feature merely to complete the story.
- **Verify:** Native screenshots, actual usage-update behavior, installation options, plan pricing, support access, included services and all example data.
- **Do not publish as facts yet:** Destination totals, activation times, “best signal”, always-on coverage, unlimited guarantees, customer counts, ratings, 24/7 support or security benefits.
- **Test:** A clarity-led first image versus a destination-led alternative once sufficient traffic exists. Change one element at a time; evaluate store install conversion and subsequent activation/purchase quality. No uplift estimate is asserted.

## A stronger long-term difference needs product proof

“Made clear” is an immediately usable presentation direction, not a claim of unique
market positioning. The supplied Openline settings design mentions automatic
network switching and ad/tracker blocking. Those mockup labels do not prove the
capabilities exist in the released service.

If engineering and supplier evidence validate network selection across multiple
carriers, that could support a more distinctive reliability-led story. First
document supported destinations, switching behavior, limitations and a repeatable
device demonstration. Security/privacy positioning likewise needs an actual SDK
and network-data audit. Until then, neither should become an unqualified store
headline. In particular, the sixth settings image contains these unverified
design labels and should remain a reference, not a final upload.
