# Openline Store Copy: Competitor Audit

## Scope and Limitations

US-English App Store and Google Play storefront copy for Saily, Holafly, and Airalo, observed 30 October 2026. This is a metadata and copy audit only, not a service test, a conversion experiment, or evidence of policy compliance by any party. Being listed on either store does not confirm that any specific claim meets current platform guidelines.

## Saily

Saily's [Apple listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/saily-esim-data-for-travel/id6475045151) opens by naming a recognisable travel frustration before explaining the product, a useful editorial model for a benefit-led opening. [The structured sections](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/saily-esim-data-for-travel/id6475045151), including a plain eSIM explainer, a traveller-persona block, and a four-step how-it-works, give a reader clear places to land. The [Google listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.saily.android&hl=en_US&gl=US) shifts toward app-workflow language rather than repeating persona framing, and its short description is direct about what the product does. [Saily's Google listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.saily.android&hl=en_US&gl=US) claims prepaid travel data in 200+ destinations and no roaming fees; these are Saily's own representations and cannot be imported as Openline facts. [The Apple listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/saily-esim-data-for-travel/id6475045151) also claims instant setup, 24/7 support, and arrival-moment activation, none of which are verified for Openline. 

## Holafly

[Holafly's Apple listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/holafly-esim-unlimited-data/id1629600786) uses named benefit categories that help a reader scan quickly, and its purchase-to-setup step sequence is clear in structure. [The Google listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.holafly.holafly&hl=en_US&gl=US) follows the same logical flow, providing consistency across platforms. [The Google listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.holafly.holafly&hl=en_US&gl=US) also uses emoji as section markers and promises unlimited internet freedom as a universal statement in its short description; the hotspot claims observed there are specific to Holafly's product and are not verified for Openline. My editorial recommendation is to borrow the section hierarchy, not the repetition.

## Airalo

[Airalo's Apple listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/airalo-esim-for-travel-data/id1475911720) includes a FAQ block that addresses simultaneous SIMs and distinguishes data-only plans from plans that include calls and SMS. [The explicit mention of a fair-usage policy for unlimited packages](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/airalo-esim-for-travel-data/id1475911720) is a useful transparency signal worth noting editorially. [The Google listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mobillium.airalo&hl=en_US&gl=US) opens at the arrival and friction moment rather than with a promotional sentence. [Airalo's listings](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/airalo-esim-for-travel-data/id1475911720) include specific customer-count figures, and reach claims; these are Airalo's own assertions and are not transferable. [The calls and SMS FAQ](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/airalo-esim-for-travel-data/id1475911720) is relevant to Airalo's product scope but outside Openline's confirmed feature set.

## Editorial Recommendation on Copy Length

The examples above suggest a useful structure: a short eSIM explainer, benefits and a step sequence. That structure addresses three distinct reader needs: understanding, evaluating, and acting. The revised Openline copy adopts the same structure at a moderately expanded length. This is an editorial judgment based on observed category norms, not a documented platform optimum or a guarantee of conversion improvement.

Price-free copy remains our design preference; this audit does not establish a trust-score or review advantage.
